ACTIVITY 1 – DESIGN FACTORS / CONSTRAINTS

What / Why / How

  • What: identify the factor or constraint.
  • Why: explain why it matters to the client, user, site or design.
  • How: explain how it changes layout, specification, structure, programme or cost.

Design Factors

  • Building use – purpose of the building drives room sizes, layout, services and circulation.
  • Spatial requirements – size of rooms, number of spaces, adjacencies, circulation space.
  • Future flexibility – can spaces be adapted, expanded or divided later?
  • Sustainability – low-carbon materials, insulation, renewables, water use, EPC.
  • Accessibility – inclusive access, Part M, ease of movement for all users.
  • Sympathetic design – fit with surrounding buildings, local character, modern vs traditional.
  • MMC – modular, off-site manufacture, prefab, SIPs, precast.

Timeframe

  • Programme of Works – planned sequence of tasks with start and finish dates.
  • Acceleration works may be needed if deadlines are tight.
  • Critical activities affect overall completion.
  • Delays may lead to liquidated damages.
  • Prefabrication / modular can reduce on-site time.

Deliveries / Logistics / Storage

  • Pre-fab reduces number of deliveries and need for on-site storage.
  • JIT deliveries help where space is limited.
  • Restricted delivery times may apply on urban or school sites.
  • Bulk buying may save cost but increases storage need.
  • Poor access can affect plant, deliveries and sequencing.

Cost

  • BoQs and QS input help control cost.
  • Contingency should be allowed for unknowns.
  • Pre-fab can reduce labour time and human error.
  • Higher spec and better quality usually increase cost.
  • Budget can limit finishes, systems and complexity.

Ground Conditions

  • Ground investigation needed before final design.
  • Water table, contamination, chemicals and made ground all affect foundations.
  • Bad ground can mean excavation to formation level, piling, more time and more money.
  • Existing underground services must be identified before handover and design finalisation.
  • Piling may reduce need for deep mass excavation but increases specialist cost.

Site / Planning Constraints

  • Site size, shape, orientation, trees, neighbouring buildings, access roads.
  • Conservation area or listed building status affects appearance and materials.
  • Planning policies may cover height, overlooking, privacy, parking and massing.
  • Flood risk may influence levels, drainage and planning approval.
  • Party wall issues may arise if close to adjoining properties.

ACTIVITY 2 – SPATIAL REQUIREMENTS

Room Sizes / Layout

  • Explain the size of specified rooms and why they are suitable.
  • Consider adjacency of spaces and flow through the building.
  • Allow enough circulation for users, furniture, equipment and maintenance.
  • Think about whether spaces can be extended or subdivided later.

Lighting / Noise

  • Maximise natural light where beneficial.
  • Avoid glare, especially on west-facing elevations.
  • Use rooflights where useful.
  • Consider noise from roads, plant, service rooms and adjacent spaces.

Building Regulations / Access

  • Part M – doors, corridors, turning circles, entrances, toilets and parking.
  • Ensure the building is usable by all.
  • Allow clear circulation space and easy navigation.
  • Accessibility should be built in, not added later.

Parking / External Areas

  • Car park size must suit building use and likely occupancy.
  • Include accessible parking and safe pedestrian routes.
  • Consider turning areas, drop-off zones and service access.

Desired Outcomes

  • Sustainability – materials, methods, insulation, CO2 reduction, renewable energy, EPC, water use.
  • Aesthetic – sympathetic, modern, traditional, context-led.
  • Accessibility – inclusive design, community benefit.
  • Futureproofing – flexible use, long-term adaptability.
  • ROI – client value, life-cycle benefit, maintenance considerations.

Methods of Construction

  • Off-site construction / prefab
  • Modular
  • SIPs
  • Precast
  • Brick and block

Always justify by time, cost, sustainability, quality control, logistics and site constraints.

Site Information

  • Deliveries / restricted access / restricted times / orientation.
  • Brownfield or greenfield.
  • Availability of existing services.
  • Ground investigation and water conditions.
  • Likely foundation solutions.

Budget Requirements

  • Contingency – around 10% can be allowed for risk/unknowns.
  • Preliminaries – around 5% depending on project.
  • Allow for extras such as solar if included.
  • Use cost per m² for early estimates.
  • Use SPONS, BoQs and QS input.
  • Early completion bonus may be offered to contractor.

ACTIVITY 3 – POSSIBLE BUILDING SIZE / FORM / STRUCTURE

Building Size / Form

  • Expand from Activity 2 spatial requirements.
  • Check floor-to-ceiling heights are suitable for building use.
  • South-facing elevations can improve daylight and passive solar gain.
  • Service rooms can be placed to the north where less daylight is needed.
  • Consider fire exits and safe escape routes.

Roof Design

  • Flat sedum roof with planted substrate over waterproof membrane.
  • Benefits: biodiversity, extra insulation, visual quality, rainwater attenuation.
  • Need slight falls for drainage and good waterproofing detail.

Foundations

  • Pad foundations – common under columns or point loads where ground is suitable.
  • Raft foundations – spread load over a larger area; useful on weaker ground.
  • Piled foundations – transfer load deeper where near-surface ground is poor.

Superstructure

  • Brick and block
  • SIPs on block
  • Steel frame with cladding / SIPs / Trespa
  • Concrete frame
  • Glass façade / panels / curtain walling
  • Modular off-site systems

Justify Foundation and Superstructure

Use this structure every time:

  • Suitability for ground conditions
  • Fit for purpose for building type and loads
  • Timeframe and speed of construction
  • Cost and budget impact
  • Sustainability
  • Storage / delivery needs
  • Noise and disruption during construction
  • Visual impact and finish
  • Maintenance and durability

Material / System Prompts

SIPs

  • Can be used for internal and external walls, roofs and sometimes separating walls depending on system/build-up.
  • Fast to erect, good thermal performance, less waste, modern method.
  • Needs careful detailing for moisture, fire and airtightness.

Brick and Block

  • Traditional, familiar, robust.
  • Can be finished with render, brick slips or cladding.
  • Usually slower than off-site systems.

Steel Frame

  • Fast erection, strong, suited to larger spans and flexible layouts.
  • Often needs fire protection and external finishing systems.
  • Can reduce on-site programme.

Concrete Frame

  • In situ concrete is durable, fire resistant, high compressive strength, high acoustic mass and low maintenance.
  • Suitable for medium to high-rise buildings.
  • Can be quick with modern methods but often needs finishes/cladding.
  • Higher embodied carbon than some alternatives.

Modular

  • Made off-site, fast installation, less waste, fewer deliveries.
  • Good where site access and programme are tight.
  • Transport and crane access must be considered.

Glass Façade / Curtain Walling

  • Strong visual impact and daylight benefit.
  • Can increase glare, solar gain and cost if not carefully designed.
  • Needs good thermal performance and maintenance planning.
